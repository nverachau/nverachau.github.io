A project index for questions about frictions, information, and who gets to respond.

The central question across the projects is how frictions change who responds to economic opportunities: knowledge moves through networks, intermediaries shape household decisions, and firm heterogeneity changes aggregate technology growth.

working paper

Technology Adoption & Network Effects: Lessons from the Energy Sector

Technology adoption is partly a knowledge-network problem.

I study how knowledge sharing affects technology adoption using the oil and gas sector. The paper separates firm incentives from spillover exposure, showing how learning networks can amplify or dampen adoption after a technology shock.

Energy finance / Innovation / Knowledge spillovers

with Greg Buchak and Adam Jorring

Integrated Intermediation & Fintech Market Power

Intermediary integration changes who captures refinancing surplus.

Using mortgage refinancing, this project studies how intermediaries with multiple roles affect borrower outcomes. Servicing relationships can change search, retention, and how fintech lenders compete.

Household finance / Fintech / Mortgage intermediation

03

working paper

Aggregate Technological Change: The Joint Effect of Firm Heterogeneity & Knowledge Spillovers

The distribution of firms determines which part of the technology frontier moves.

In the presence of knowledge spillovers, the distribution of firm types becomes an input into aggregate technology improvement. Using recentered influence function regressions, I provide empirical evidence for these differences in the American oil and gas industry.

Corporate finance / Firm heterogeneity / Technology growth

Other Work

Robust Regimes: The Direct Impact of Model Uncertainty

Hansen-Sargent models of robustness consider agents who are concerned with model misspecification. This project proposes a method to estimate how model uncertainty affects forecasts and real options decisions.
